## What was counted

The objects are `build/release/Core/CMakeFiles/lxr_dsp.dir/Src/Soundengine/DSPAudio/...`
(`Reverb`, `AllpassDelay`, `VerbDelay`, `VerbModDelay`, `sinCos`, `TiltEq`, `1PoleLp`), built with
`-O3 -ffast-math -freciprocal-math -mfp16-format=ieee`, Cortex-M7, no LTO (AF12). They were
disassembled with `arm-none-eabi-objdump -d --no-show-raw-insn` into `build/a1/*.dis`.
`TiltEq_process` and `calcOnePole` are real functions (in `TiltEq.c.obj` and `1PoleLp.c.obj`), not
inlined, so they appear as callees.

`<Reverb_process>`: the sample loop runs from `0xc4` to the function's last backward conditional
branch, `bcc.w c4` at `0x2fc`. Everything before `0xc4` and after `0x2fc` (prologue, the per-block
LFO update, epilogue) is I_out.

**Callees.** The loop's callees call further functions (`AllpassDelay_process` calls
`VerbDelay_read` and `VerbDelay_write`; `AllpassModDelay_process` calls `VerbModDelay_calcLength`,
`VerbModDelay_read` and `VerbModDelay_write`; `VerbModDelay_calcLength` calls `calcOnePole`). The
table therefore counts every function reached from the loop, with n_f its calls per iteration over
the whole call tree; each call is charged I_f + 4. I_f is every instruction from the label to the
last return (`bx lr`, `pop {…, pc}`, `ldr.w pc, [sp], #4`), literal words and trailing `nop` left
out; `VerbModDelay_calcLength` is the only callee with a branch, and all its instructions are
counted (the longest path).

Σ n_f·(I_f + 4) = 816.

**Buffer accesses (N_buf)** are the loads and stores of the fp16 delay lines: `ldrh`/`strh` with a
register-scaled index (`[rB, rI, lsl #1]`), each with its `vcvtb` next to it, counted once per
access. The loop body itself has none; the four `ldrsh` of the input block and the `ldr`/`str` of
the output pair are 16-bit audio-bus accesses, not delay lines, and are counted only in I_loop.
**Divisions (N_div):** no `vdiv` or `vsqrt` in the loop or in any callee (the two `vdiv` of
`TiltEq.c.obj` are in `TiltEq_setFreq`/`TiltEq_setParameter`, which the loop does not call).

I_out counts the instructions of `Reverb_process` outside the loop, as the plan defines it. The
calls outside the loop (`SineCosine_calc`, `SineCosine_getSine`, `SineCosine_getCosine`, two
`VerbModDelay_setModValue`; I_f 8, 2, 2, 29, 29) would add 70 + 5·4 = 90 per block, 0.03 % of a
block: left out, as the formula says, and well inside its 1.5 factor.

## Result

With `CPUMETER_BLOCK_CYCLES` = 313,185 (64 samples at 44,140 Hz, 216 MHz; AF12):

- **R\*_full = 1.5 × 100 × (E + 64·Δ) / 313,185 = 1.5 × 100 × 73,217 / 313,185 = 35.07 %**
- **R\*_half = R\*_full / 2 + 0.5 = 18.03 %**
- R_dj = 2.0 % until A6 counts the DJ filter.
- A6 (v0.4 `d4fe079`) counted it: C_sample 27 (steady HP loop), I_out 786, **R_dj = 1.5 × 100 × (4 × 64 × 27 + 786) / 313,185 = 3.69 %**; with it, full rate if pk ≤ 46.24, half rate if pk ≤ 63.28.

Without the factor 1.5, the reverb with the v0.4 additions is 23.4 % of a block at one instruction
per cycle (E alone: 21.6 %).

**D41's rule with these numbers** (P = the highest `pk` of H12 step 3, the heavy pattern with the
delay on):

- **full rate if pk ≤ 85 − R\*_full − 2.0 = 85 − 35.07 − 2.0 = 47.93;**
- **half rate if pk ≤ 85 − R\*_half − 2.0 = 85 − 18.03 − 2.0 = 64.97;**
- **else half rate and lean** (no early reflections, mono wet signal).

A7 applies this with the owner's recorded `pk`, then re-checks R_dj from A6's count in place of 2.0.

## Stack

`Reverb_process`'s prologue is `stmdb sp!, {r4–r11, lr}` (36 B), `vpush {d8–d12}` (40 B) and
**`sub sp, #76`** (76 B): a frame of **152 B**. Its deepest call chain adds
`AllpassModDelay_process` (`push {r3, r4, r5, lr}` + `vpush {d8}`, 24 B) and
`VerbModDelay_calcLength` (`push {r4, lr}`, 8 B; `calcOnePole` uses no stack): **184 B** in all
while the reverb runs. The DTCM limit of D30 leaves at least 23,172 B for the stack.

## Notes

- The count is of fw 1.7's code at `a9865dc`, before A2's fix at `Reverb.c:375`. The fix only
  changes which `apDelay` the call receives (`apDelay[0]` instead of `apDelay[1]`), so the number
  of calls and instructions per sample stays the same (at most a register move differs).
- fw 1.7 pointed the reverb at `FX_BUFFER` in DTCM (no wait states). v0.4 moves its 14 units to
  `REVERB_RAM` in RAM (D31), behind the D-cache. The 14 lines are read and written sequentially, so a
  32-byte cache line serves 16 consecutive fp16 samples of one unit; the 2 cycles charged per buffer
  access (74 per sample) stand for those refills.
- This is a static estimate. U20 (the CPU page with the reverb on) is the measurement, and D41's
  fix-up (`pk` ≥ 95 → the next mode down) applies to it.
