## 1. The problem

A step's own timing is coarse. The shift moves it by ±7 units of 12 ticks, 1/8 of a step: 15.6 ms
at 120 BPM, 13.2 ms at 142. The shuffle is one value per pattern, for every odd 16th of every
track. Humanize is random, and the track delay is constant. Since v0.5 every voice starts at the
exact sample of its event, so finer offsets would now be heard as set.

The reference measurement (`build/reference/album/report.md` §4, `templates/<n>-top16-steps.txt`)
shows a per-position groove. In its track 5, at 142 BPM, the 16ths right after each quarter
(steps 2, 6, 10, 14) are 16–26 ms late, and those before the quarter (4, 8, 12, 16) 2–12 ms. The
quarters and the offbeat 8ths sit on the grid, and so does the kick. Neither the shuffle nor the
shift can play that.

**Goal.**
- **E6.4:** each step gets its own offset, at a resolution of one tick (1/96 of a step: 1.30 ms at
  120 BPM, 1.10 ms at 142).
- **E6.2:** a track can follow a groove template, one offset per 16th of the bar, scaled by an amount.
- **E6.5:** live recording stores the played offset instead of quantizing it away.
- With no template and no microtiming, `trg smp` plays as v0.5 and `trg blk` as v0.4, bit for bit.

## 2. Facts (checked at `a213a5d`)

Line numbers refer to the base commit. Facts marked *measured* come from the v0.5 release build in
`build/wt-v05/build/release/` (its linker map and `arm-none-eabi-objdump -d`).

- **MF1. The step data.**
  - `Step` is stored raw, 7 bytes (`SeqStepTypes.h:32–44`).
  - `StepTiming` is one byte (`:47–51`): `flam` in bits 0–3 (0..5 give 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 hits; any
    other value gives 1), `shift` in bits 4–7 (0..14 = −7..+7 units, 15 counts as 0)
    (`seq_core.c:421–435`).
  - No bits are free for a fine offset. Every bit of `Step` reaches an output: `note` goes into the
    MIDI Note On, the p-lock values go to the CC handler, and bit 7 of `prob` is v0.3's slide. The
    unused codes (flam 6..15, shift 15) amount to less than two bits.
- **MF2. Patterns in RAM.** `PatternSet seq_patternSet` (`sequencer.h:97–107`) holds all 64
  patterns × 7 tracks × 64 steps. That is `Step` 200,704 B, `StepTiming` 28,672, the main steps
  3,584, the lengths 448, shuffle 64, kit numbers 64 and `groove` 1,792: 235,328 B (*measured*:
  `.bss.seq_patternSet` 0x39740). Pattern changes, copies, recording and edits never touch the card.
  `patternBackupBuffer` (3,678 B) keeps the active pattern as loaded, for the revert on a pattern
  change.
- **MF3. The shift.** A shift unit is L/8 ticks in a track step of L ticks: 12 at ×1, exact at all
  11 tempos. The tick base is 96 per step, 384 PPQN (`seq_core.h:231`). `shf` sits on the step
  page's second screen (`menuPages.h:453–454`) with a 15-entry name list (`MenuText.h:82`). The
  menu's message reaches `CortexParser.c:750`, which writes `.shift` raw.
- **MF4. The offset now** (`seqc_offset()`, `seq_core.c:1105`):
  - offset = swing (odd `absStep`) + shift + min(delay + humanize, L), floored at −(L − L/8);
  - delay and humanize are whole ticks at the current tempo, rounded half away from zero
    (`seqc_msTicks()`, `:1034`);
  - hit j of a ratchet lies at base + offset + floor(j·L/k);
  - hits before n·96 form the step's early part, generated at boundary n−1 (N3). The slot keeps the
    early part's offset (`seqc_draw_t.offset`, int16) for the late part.
- **MF5. Rolls (v0.3).** `seqc_queueRoll()` (`:1227`) places roll hits at the step boundary plus the
  swing, without shift, delay or humanize. They are generated at the boundary itself, so they cannot
  lie before it.
- **MF6. Firing** (`seq_core.h:171–189`).
  - Choke per track: firing an event of (t, n) removes the queued events of t with a lower `absStep`.
  - At one tick only the highest n fires.
  - The note-off comes 4 ticks after the trigger, and always before the track's next trigger.
  - Tracks 6 and 7 share the hi-hat voice. v0.5 applies a voice's records within one block at the
    last one's offset (S2).
- **MF7. v0.5's times.** `seq_cbTrigger()` posts the voice record at `seqc_itemTime()` =
  `seqc_internalTime(ev->tick)` (`sequencer.c:2040–2048`). MIDI Note On, LED and roll recording
  happen at fire time (S8). A change of `ev->tick` therefore moves the voice and the MIDI note
  together. `seqc_event_t` has one spare byte, `reserved` (`seq_core.h:359`).
- **MF8. Recording (v0.1 D6).**
  - `seq_addNote()` (`sequencer.c:1330`) writes into the current track step. From a phase of 0.5 on
    it writes into the next step and sets that step's skip-once marker.
  - The phase is `seqc_trackPhase()` (`seq_core.c:3048`) as of the last `seq_tick()`. It reaches 0.5
    halfway between the swung grid times.
  - There are three callers:
    - MIDI Note On (`MidiParser.c:1546`), inside `seq_tick()`, with v0.5's arrival stamp at `:1536`;
    - the mailbox's one-shot roll (`sequencer.c:2365`);
    - roll hits, which record at their own step (`:2068`).
  - `seq_recordNoteAtStep()` sets note, velocity, prob 100 and the main step. It leaves
    `StepTiming` alone.
- **MF9. Pattern files.**
  - `.pat` holds the name (8), the tag `PatV03` (6), 7 × (448 `Step` + 64 `StepTiming`), 56 main
    step bytes, shuffle, 7 lengths and the kit number: 3,663 B.
  - v0.2's block 1 follows: `LCHM`, 1, 7, then 7 × `TrackGroove`, 34 B
    (`presetManager.c:329–362`, `:1747–1795`).
  - `tmp.pat` has the pattern number instead of name and tag: 3,650 + 34 B.
  - The v0.2–v0.5 reader stops after block 1, and on any mismatch it leaves the defaults.
- **MF10. fw 1.7's reader** (`upstream/main`, `presetManager.c:2055–2072`, `:1894`, `:1962`).
  - It takes the tag's digits with `atoi`: above 2 it reads the timing bytes, above 1 the kit
    number, and then it closes the file.
  - It never sees a byte after the kit number (v0.2 F2), and a pattern it saves has no block.
- **MF11. The menu.**
  - A sub-page has 8 slots, 4 per screen. The encoder crosses from slot 8 into the next sub-page
    only on `SEQ_PAGE` and `MENU_MIDI_PAGE` (`menu.c:3197–3215`).
  - The step page is `vol nte prb sld | flm shf – –`; slots 7–8 are `TEXT_SKIP`, and the second
    sub-page holds the p-locks.
  - The track (Euklid) page has one sub-page, `len stp rot mrp | tmp hmt hmv dly`
    (`menuPages.h:466`).
  - SHIFT + REC opens the recording page (`buttonHandler.c:1998`): `aut` and seven empty slots.
  - In edit mode the top row shows category and long name, and the bottom row shows the value at
    column 13 (`menu.c:1576`).
- **MF12. The menu's messages to the sequencer are MIDI-framed.** A byte with bit 7 set starts a
  message (`frontPanelParser.c:150`), so a value is 0..127. v0.2's track settings bypass them with
  direct calls: `seq_setGrooveParam()` (`menu.c:3607`) and `seq_loadGrooveParams()`
  (`sequencer.c:2518`).
- **MF13. Parameter numbers.**
  - `UNUSED_PAR_GLO_SAT` (`Parameters.h:370`), in the sequencer range, appears nowhere but in the
    dtype table. The sequencer range is never saved.
  - `PAR_TRIG_GATE_MODE` (`:412`) is glo.cfg byte 19. fw 1.7 sets it to 0
    (`presetManager.c:1224`), its handler is commented out (`CortexParser.c:1136`), and no page
    lists it.
  - `activeParameters` (309 B, RAM) is followed by 3 B of fill (*measured*).
  - `ParameterArray.h` holds a second copy of the enum (v0.4 AF6).
- **MF14. Memory** (*measured*).
  - RAM: `.data` 864 + `.bss` 344,912 + heap 512 = 346,288 B.
  - DTCM: `.dtcm` 36,996 + `.dtcmz` 70,400 = 107,396 B. The sum is 453,684, and ITCM holds 13,728.
  - The limits (D30): the sum ≤ 455,544, a bookkeeping rule (fw 1.7's 378,420 plus named
    additions); DTCM ≤ 107,900, which protects the stack; RAM ≤ 389,120, the physical check
    (393,216 less the top 4 KB below `_estack`).
  - RAM therefore has 42,832 B of physical headroom.
- **MF15. CPU** (*measured*).
  - `seqc_generate()` is 676 instructions with 4 divisions in the release object, with
    `seqc_offset()`, `seqc_draw()` and `seqc_msTicks()` inlined.
  - `seq_core` has no ITCM code.
  - `seq_tick()` runs right before the render, and the CPU meter counts both (v0.5 SF1).
- **MF16. Tests.**
  - There are 32 goldens: v0.4's 23, then G16–G19 and five `_blk` copies from v0.5. None records
    notes, and no card carries a block 2.
  - `M17_seed_card` expects 194 files of 258,420 B.
  - `tools/emu_onsets.py` measures onsets against the trigger log, the tick grid, or another render.

## 4. Timing

### 4.1 One step's offset

For an occurrence of track t at `absStep` n, read at (pattern p, step s), in a track step of L ticks,
with loop count ℓ and track length N:

```
C = (shift − 7) · L/8              shift 0..14; 15 counts as 0          (v0.5, exact)
F = rha(fine · L / 96)             fine −6..+5                          (M1, M3)
q = (ℓ · N + s) mod 16                                                 (M6)
G = rha(tpl[id][q] · (amount + 100) · L / 9600)   0 if id = 0           (M4, M5)
P = clamp(C + F + G, −7L/8, +7L/8)                                     (M7)
S = swing(shuffle, L) on odd n, else 0                                 (v0.2)
D = min(msTicks(delay) + jitter, L)                                    (v0.2)
offset = max(S + P + D, −7L/8)                                         (v0.2)
hit j  = base + offset + floor(j · L / k)                              (v0.3)
```

`rha` rounds half away from zero, as `seqc_msTicks()` does. At ×1 (L = 96) F = fine and C = 12·(shift
− 7), so P = T + G. If F = G = 0, then P = C, and |C| ≤ 7L/8, so the clamp does nothing: the events
are v0.5's, tick for tick.

**The order.** The step's own position comes first: shift, fine and template, clamped together to
the shift's reach. Then the swing is added, then delay and humanize (at most one step later, v0.2),
and the whole is floored at 7/8 of a step earlier. The latest a hit can come stays v0.5's:
S + 7L/8 + L, 244 ticks at ×1. v0.6 adds no reach.

### 4.2 Templates

Offsets in ticks at 100 %, positions 1–16 from the downbeat. The measured values are the `onset_ms`
column of `templates/<n>-top16-steps.txt`, converted at the track's BPM (1 tick = 60,000 / (BPM ·
384) ms, so 1.1004 ms at 142 and 1.0927 at 143). They are rounded half away from zero, and the
quarters are set to 0 (the kick is the grid).

- **`rlx`.** Track 7 at 143 BPM: 15.16, 1.24, 3.22, 16.49, 1.65, 22.48, 14.40, 1.71, 16.12, 9.12,
  2.44, 17.57.
- **`2bt`.** Track 6: beats 1 and 3 averaged (e 6.81, & 2.93, a 8.18), beats 2 and 4 averaged
  (e 0.68, & 2.49, a 1.43).

### 4.3 Interactions

- **Shift.** It is now the coarse view of `mic` (M14). A v0.5 step with `shf` +2 shows `mic` +24.
- **Shuffle** is unchanged: per pattern, on odd `absStep`, added to P. A template and the shuffle
  add up. Groove work is cleaner with the shuffle at 0 and a template on the tracks that should
  move.
- **Humanize and delay** are unchanged. They are added after P (§4.1), and humanize draws once per
  occurrence (v0.2).
- **Track tempo.** F and G are fractions of the track's own step, like C. At ×3/4 (L = 128) a fine
  of +5 is 7 ticks; at ×4 (L = 24) the fine values −6..+5 become −2..+1. A template on a track at ×2
  spreads its 16 positions over half a bar of the master.
- **Ratchets.** All hits move with the first one. Hits that fall at or after the next step's first
  hit are removed when that step fires (MF6).
- **The next step, and the "+90 %" case.** `mic` stops at +84 (87.5 % of a step), and P is clamped
  there whatever the template adds. +84 at ×1 puts the hit 12 ticks before the next step's grid
  time, a flam of 15.6 ms at 120 BPM, and both hits play. If the swing (odd steps) or a delay pushes
  it past the next step's first hit, that hit fires first and its choke drops the late one (M9,
  counted in `chokeDrops`). On one track, a step therefore never sounds after the next step's
  first hit. Its note-off still comes before the next trigger (MF6).
- **Shared hi-hat (tracks 6 and 7).** Whichever triggers later wins the voice. A closed hat moved
  after an open hat of the same step cuts it. Within one block, v0.5 applies both records in order at
  the later offset (S2), so the later one is heard.
- **Rolls.** M8. With a template on a track, a held roll plays on the swung straight grid.
- **Edits while playing.** The fine part belongs to the step's timing, and N3's rule applies to it
  as to the shift: an early part's offset is kept for its late part. A template or amount edit acts
  from the next generated step on, like a shuffle edit.
- **Pattern change, song position, slave sync.** Queued events keep their ticks (D5). q restarts with
  ℓ and s. In slave modes the ticks follow the incoming tempo, so templates scale with it.

### 4.4 Outputs

- **Voice:** `seqc_itemTime()` of the moved tick (MF7). v0.5 is unchanged.
- **MIDI Note On and Off:** at the offset time (M10). The Note Off comes 4 ticks after the Note On.
- **MIDI clock, the clock out jack, LEDs, chase light:** unchanged, on the master grid.

## 7. Recording (E6.5)

### 7.1 The time and the place of a note

`seq_addNote()` gets the note's time t (M21):
- the MIDI parser passes the stamp it already rebuilds for the voice (`vsched_stampTime()`);
- the mailbox's pad roll passes `seqc_itemTime()`, which outside a callback is the core's time.

It then calls `seqc_recordPlace(track, t, &out)` (M28). The call is allowed inside `seq_tick()` only,
where both callers run (MF8). That function:
1. takes x = `seqc_positionAt(t)` in ticks. In the slave modes this is the core's interpolation, so
   recording follows an external clock;
2. computes the reference times of the track's current step c and its next step c+1 (`(c + 1) mod
   N`, in the active pattern, as now): r = grid + S + G + D (§4.1, with each step's own q and swing,
   and humanize left out);
3. picks the nearer one, with a tie going to c+1 (M20). x may lie a little before c's boundary when
   the note is parsed after it; c is still the nearer candidate;
4. returns `ahead` (0 or 1) and µ = clamp(rha((x − r) · 96 / L), −84, +84).

It returns false while stopped, armed or before the first step, and nothing is recorded then, as in
v0.5.

### 7.2 What is stored

- **`rqn off`:** `seq_recordNoteAtStep()` writes note, velocity, prob 100 and the main step as now,
  and also the step's coarse shift and fine part from µ (M3). The flam, the p-locks and the step's
  other bits stay. For `ahead` = 1 the skip-once marker is set (D6), so the step does not play again
  when the sequencer reaches it.
- **`rqn on`:** the same step choice, and the step's timing untouched: v0.5's recording, except that
  the choice uses the note's own time and includes the template and the delay.
- **The rounding** is half away from zero, to one tick of the track's step: an error of at most 0.65
  ms at 120 BPM and 0.55 at 142. The MIDI stamp adds less than 0.01 ms.
- **With a template active**, the stored µ is the played position minus the template (M19). So a
  note played on the groove stores 0, and the step's `mic` shows how far the player was from the
  groove. The same goes for the swing and the delay.
- **Overdub.** LXR recording always overdubs. A note on a step that is already on replaces its
  note, velocity, prob and (with `rqn off`) its timing. Two notes on one step in one pass: the later
  one wins. The erase mode is unchanged.
- **Rolls.** Roll hits record as in v0.5, at their own step with the timing left alone.
- **The edge kept from v0.5.** If a recorded step already holds a hit that is queued but has not
  fired (a positive offset), both the live note and that hit sound once. The skip-once marker covers
  only the next step.
- The phase copy `seq_phaseCopy` and its refresh in `seq_refreshCopies()` serve only recording
  (MF8). The plan removes them if nothing else reads them (−28 B, not counted in §9).

## 8. CPU cost

The method is v0.5's (§6.1): a static instruction count, at one cycle per instruction, plus 13 per
division and 4 per call, times D41's factor 1.5, over 313,185 cycles per block. The base is
`seqc_generate()` (MF15).

| Part, per `seqc_generate()` call that reaches the offset | Cycles |
|---|---:|
| `fine()` and `tmpl()` through the host pointers, with their bodies (index, nibble, sign, check) | 2 · (4 + 9) = 26 |
| template: q, table read, v · pct · L, one division, rounding | 4 + 3 + 3 + 13 + 5 = 28 |
| fine: f · L, one division, rounding (at L = 96: 2) | 3 + 13 + 5 = 21 |
| sum and clamp to ±7L/8 | 8 |
| **Total** | **≈ 83** |

| Case | Calls in the block | Raw | With 1.5 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **Worst:** all 7 tracks step at one boundary (late part of n, early part of n+1) | 14 | 0.37 % | **0.56 %** |
| The same with fine 0 and templates `off` (the callbacks return early, ≈ 30 per call) | 14 | 0.13 % | 0.20 % |
| Mean, 7 tracks of 16ths at 142 BPM (a step every 72.8 blocks) | | | ≈ 0.008 % |
| One recorded note (`seqc_recordPlace()`: `seqc_positionAt()` 49, two reference times with `seqc_msTicks()` in double, the rounding; ≈ 250) | | 0.08 % | 0.12 %, once per note |

- **vsched does not change.** Microtiming adds no records. A hit off the block grid already costs a
  phase split per sounding voice and block (v0.5 S13), and almost every hit is off the block grid.
  The mean `CPU` therefore stays where v0.5 put it.
- **D41 (M25).** v0.5 bounds `pk` by 85 + 4.64 = 89.64. A boundary block can coincide with the worst
  hit block, so v0.6's bound is 89.64 + 0.56 = 90.20, still below U20's 95. U37 measures it.
- **Flash** grows by about 3 KB: code, the 128-byte table and the names. ITCM does not change
  (MF15).

## 12. Risks

- **Bit-identity rests on P = C** when fine is 0 and the template `off`. Three things guard it: the
  `queue_equiv` comparison, the 32 unchanged goldens and the `_blk` copies. One rounding or clamp in
  the wrong place would change hit times by a tick, and the goldens would show it.
- **The templates come from one album's averaged envelopes.** The weaker steps are smeared and
  partly unreliable (report §4, `~`), and the per-bar medians are larger. `amt` is the correction, and
  U34 lets the owner judge. The numbers stay in one table (§4.2), so they can be changed without a
  format change.
- **The knob's resolution for `mic`** (169 values on a knob of about 128 positions). The encoder
  steps one tick. If the knob jumps feel wrong, the plan can make knob 3 coarse (12 ticks) and leave
  the encoder fine.
- **Menu values above 127.** Any path that still sends `mic` or `amt` through the menu's messages
  would cut them to 7 bits (MF12). `record_micro` and the `groove` card's UI path cover the direct
  calls.
- **RAM.** 15.5 KB leaves 27,360 B for later stages. A larger stage after this one would need a
  layout change, e.g. a shared buffer.
- **`NUM_PARAMS` grows to 311.** The two `isAll` padding sites are believed dead (v0.4 crossed them).
  The plan confirms it by reading, not by assumption.
- **Recording near a boundary.** A note parsed long after its arrival (a USB burst of several ms)
  can only land on the current or the next step. A note more than half a step late would land on
  the wrong step. Parse delays are milliseconds, so this needs a stalled main loop.
- **q differs from the swing's parity** in patterns of odd length: the template restarts with the
  pattern, while the swing flips each loop as before. This is by design (M6) and stated in the
  release notes.
- **Templates are in ticks**, so the same template is 18 % longer in ms at 120 BPM than at 142. That
  is the shift's and the shuffle's behaviour too.

## 13. Out of scope

- User templates: taking a track's microtiming as a template, or loading templates from the card.
  The table already has room for ids 8..255.
- Velocity templates (the measured accent profile, report §4).
- A quantized MIDI out next to the grooved one.
- A clear target or a "quantize track" function that zeroes a track's microtiming.
- Sub-tick offsets through the event's spare byte (MF7).
- Microtiming and templates for roll hits.
- One template for all tracks of a pattern in a single step.
